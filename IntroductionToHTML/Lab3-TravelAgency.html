Discovering Bihar

Bihar offers rich travel opportunities through its unique mix of history, spirituality, culture, and nature. It is a major destination for spiritual tourism with places like Bodh Gaya (where Lord Buddha attained enlightenment), Nalanda (ancient world-famous university), Rajgir, and Vaishali. History lovers can explore Patna Sahib, Golghar, and archaeological sites linked to the Maurya and Gupta empires. The state also has natural attractions such as Valmiki Tiger Reserve, river landscapes along the Ganga, and vibrant cultural experiences through local festivals, food, and traditions, making Bihar a meaningful and enriching travel destination.

Top Itineraries

  1. Patna Museum

    2. Patna Museum is the oldest state museum in Bihar, founded in 1917 and housed in an Indo-Saracenic architectural building in Patna, showcasing a rich collection of archaeological artifacts, sculptures, coins, and historical objects from ancient to colonial India. It includes rare exhibits like the Lohanipur torso and displays Buddhist, Gupta, and Mughal period relics that reflect the region’s cultural heritage. The museum continues to be a major attraction for history enthusiasts and tourists exploring Bihar’s past.

  2. Patna Ganga Aarti

    3. The Ganga Aarti at Gandhi Ghat in Patna is a captivating evening ritual performed on the banks of the sacred Ganges River, where priests chant mantras and offer lit lamps, creating a peaceful, devotional atmosphere. This spiritual ceremony draws both locals and visitors who come to witness the lights, chants, and river vistas as the sun sets. Gandhi Ghat also has historical importance as the site where Mahatma Gandhi’s ashes were immersed, adding cultural depth to the experience

  3. Bodhgaya - Land where Buddha was born

    4. Bodh Gaya is a sacred pilgrimage town in Bihar where Siddhartha Gautama attained enlightenment under the Bodhi Tree, making it one of the most important sites in Buddhism. It centers around the UNESCO-listed Mahabodhi Temple complex, attracting devotees and tourists from around the world for spiritual reflection and meditation. The site also features stupas, monasteries, and serene gardens that reflect centuries of religious history and cultural significance
